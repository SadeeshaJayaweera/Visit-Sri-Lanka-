Dambulla Cave Temple (Golden Temple)

The best-preserved cave temple complex in Sri Lanka, housing 153 Buddha statues and 2,100 square meters of painted ceiling murals across five sacred caves.

Ancient Cities
Matale District
Best Season: Early morning (07:00 - 09:30) or late afternoon (16:00 - 18:00) when the granite rock surface is comfortable to walk on barefoot.
Duration: 2 - 3 Hours
Popular Highlight

About Dambulla Cave Temple (Golden Temple)

Perched on a massive black granite outcrop rising 160 meters above the surrounding plains, the Golden Temple of Dambulla is the largest and best-preserved rock cave temple complex in Sri Lanka. Dating back to the 1st century BCE, King Valagamba (Vattagamani Abhaya) took refuge in these caves for 14 years after being driven from Anuradhapura by South Indian invaders; upon regaining his throne, he transformed the natural caverns into a magnificent rock monastery. Across five distinct sanctuaries carved beneath a towering 150-meter rock overhang, visitors behold an astonishing treasury of Buddhist art: 153 Buddha statues, including a colossal 14-meter reclining Buddha carved directly from the living rock in Cave 1 (Devaraja Lena), alongside 2,100 square meters of vibrant ceiling and wall frescoes depicting the life of the Buddha and historic battles.

Historical & Cultural Background

Consecrated as a Buddhist monastery in the 1st century BCE by King Valagamba; expanded by King Nissanka Malla in 1190 CE; inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage site in 1991.

Natural & Geographical Characteristics

Immense granite rock inselberg towering 160 meters over the surrounding dry forest.

What Makes It Special

Five monumental cave sanctuaries, 14-meter rock-carved reclining Buddha, miraculous dripping water vessel in Cave 2, and 2,100 m² of murals.

Why You Shouldn't Miss This Place

  • Step inside five magnificent ancient rock cave temples filled with golden statues and murals
  • Marvel at the 14-meter rock-cut reclining Buddha in the Cave of the Divine King
  • Witness the mysterious natural phenomenon of water dripping upwards into a golden vessel
  • Enjoy panoramic views extending towards Sigiriya Rock from the temple's rock terrace

Things to Do & Available Experiences

Cave temple exploration
Buddhist art appreciation
Panoramic viewpoint photography
Rock terrace walking

Insider Travel Tips

What to Bring: Modest clothing, socks to protect feet from hot courtyard stone, camera (no flash inside caves), water.
Recommended Clothing: Strict dress code: shoulders and knees must be covered; hats and shoes must be deposited at the shoe counter.
Photography Advice: Photograph the dramatic rows of seated Buddhas illuminated by gentle interior lighting; never pose with back turned to statues.
Local Customs: Show deep reverence to monks and worshippers inside the active sacred caves.
Eco & Conservation Guidelines: Never touch the ancient painted murals or rock carvings.

Visitor Information

District Matale District
Province Central Province
Opening Hours 07:00 - 19:00 Daily (ticket counter closes at 17:00).
Entrance Fee LKR 2,000 for foreign adult visitors (ticket office is at the base near the modern Golden Temple museum).
Best Season Early morning (07:00 - 09:30) or late afternoon (16:00 - 18:00) when the granite rock surface is comfortable to walk on barefoot.
Visiting Duration 2 - 3 Hours
Accessibility Ascending the rock requires walking up gentle stone staircases (approx. 200-300 steps); inside the caves, ground is flat.
Safety Advice Granite rock courtyard can become hot under midday sun; socks are recommended for walking on the stone terrace.
