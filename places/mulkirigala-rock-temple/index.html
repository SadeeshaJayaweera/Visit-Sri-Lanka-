Mulkirigala Raja Maha Vihara Rock Monastery

The 'Little Sigiriya' of the south, an ancient 2,000-year-old rock monastery featuring seven cave temples across five tiers rising 205 meters above the forest.

Ancient Cities
Hambantota District
Best Season: Early morning (07:30 - 10:00) or late afternoon (15:30 - 17:30) when stone steps are comfortable for barefoot walking.
Duration: 2 - 3 Hours
Hidden Gem

About Mulkirigala Raja Maha Vihara Rock Monastery

Rising 205 meters (673 feet) sheer out of the surrounding southern coconut forests near Tangalle, Mulkirigala Raja Maha Vihara (often called 'Little Sigiriya') is a majestic ancient rock cave temple. Dating back to the reign of King Saddhatissa in the 3rd century BCE, the temple complex is carved into a massive isolated granite boulder. Climbers ascend 533 stone steps carved into the rock, passing through five distinct tiered terraces housing seven historic cave temples. Within these caverns survive astonishing Kandyan-era ceiling murals, rock-cut reclining Buddhas up to 14 meters long, and an ancient pool. It was here in 1826 that British colonial scholar George Turnour discovered the missing palm-leaf ola leaf 'Tika' (commentary) to the Mahavamsa (the Great Chronicle of Sri Lanka), which enabled the translation of ancient Sri Lankan and Indian history for the first time.

Historical & Cultural Background

Founded in the 3rd century BCE by King Saddhatissa; played a crucial role in world scholarship when the Mahavamsa key was discovered here in 1826.

Natural & Geographical Characteristics

Isolated sheer granite inselberg towering 205 meters above lowland coconut and palmyra woodlands.

What Makes It Special

Five ascending rock tiers, seven cave temples with reclining Buddhas, and the Mahavamsa translation discovery site.

Why You Shouldn't Miss This Place

  • Climb through five ascending rock terraces with ancient cave temples and vibrant murals
  • Stand at the historical site where the translation key to Sri Lanka's Mahavamsa was discovered
  • Marvel at 14-meter rock-cut reclining Buddha statues inside cool natural caverns
  • Enjoy sweeping 360-degree views over southern forests and distant ocean from the summit stupa

Things to Do & Available Experiences

Rock monastery ascent (533 steps)
Ancient mural and statue appreciation
Panoramic viewpoint photography
Historical scholarship discovery tour

Insider Travel Tips

What to Bring: Modest clothing, socks (to wear on hot stone terraces), drinking water, camera.
Recommended Clothing: Shoulders and knees must be respectfully covered.
Photography Advice: Photograph the panoramic view from the summit bell-shaped stupa looking across endless coconut groves.
Local Customs: Quiet, sacred atmosphere; friendly resident Buddhist monks.
Eco & Conservation Guidelines: Never touch ancient painted murals on cave walls.
