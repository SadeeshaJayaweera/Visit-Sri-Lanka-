Jetavanaramaya Stupa

The world's tallest ancient brick monument, a staggering 122-meter stupa containing over 93 million baked clay bricks, built by King Mahasena in the 3rd century CE.

Ancient Cities
Anuradhapura District

Popular Highlight

About Jetavanaramaya Stupa

Standing with colossal architectural mass in the eastern quarter of ancient Anuradhapura, Jetavanaramaya is an engineering marvel that astonished the ancient world. Built in the late 3rd century CE (circa 273–301 CE) by King Mahasena, this stupa was originally the third tallest structure in the ancient world at 122 meters (400 feet)—surpassed only by the Great Pyramids of Giza. It remains to this day the largest and tallest brick monument ever constructed by human hands on planet Earth, containing an estimated 93.3 million baked clay bricks. Dedicated to the Sagalika Buddhist sect, the stupa was built over a relic believed to be a part of the Buddha's sash (belt/patakada). The surrounding 5.6-hectare monastic university once housed over 3,000 monks, featuring grand refectories, stone baths, and massive granite image houses.

Historical & Cultural Background

Built by King Mahasena in the 3rd century CE; historically the largest brick building ever constructed; partially restored by the Central Cultural Fund.

Natural & Geographical Characteristics

Vast reddish-terracotta baked brick dome rising majestically out of peaceful dry forest parkland.

What Makes It Special

World's tallest ancient brick monument (originally 122m, currently 71m), 93.3 million bricks, and the Jetavana Museum.

Why You Shouldn't Miss This Place

  • Stand before the single largest brick monument constructed in human history
  • Marvel at ancient 3rd-century engineering that rivaled the Great Pyramids of Giza in scale
  • Touch ancient baked bricks inscribed with names of ancient Buddhist donors
  • Visit the adjacent Jetavana Museum exhibiting Roman coins, Greek pottery, and delicate gems

Things to Do & Available Experiences

Stupa terrace walking
Brick engineering study
Visiting the Jetavana Archaeological Museum
Historical photography

Insider Travel Tips

What to Bring: Socks to wear on the terrace, sun hat, camera, drinking water.
Recommended Clothing: Modest clothing covering shoulders and knees.
Photography Advice: Step back into the surrounding forest park to capture the full, immense curvature of the terracotta dome.
Local Customs: Quiet, contemplative historical atmosphere.
Eco & Conservation Guidelines: Strict preservation of ancient masonry; do not litter.
