Ratnapura City of Gems & Traditional Mines

The world's premier gem trading capital, where traditional bamboo-shored pit mines unearth the world's finest royal blue sapphires, rubies, and alexandrites.

About Ratnapura City of Gems & Traditional Mines

Renowned worldwide for over 2,500 years as the 'City of Gems' (from the Sanskrit Ratna-pura), Ratnapura is the historical center of the global sapphire trade. The fertile alluvial floodplains and gravels (illam) beneath this valley have yielded some of the world's most famous and priceless gemstones, including the 423-carat 'Logan Blue Sapphire', the 'Star of India' (563 carats), and the blue sapphire in the British Royal Family's engagement ring worn by Princess Diana and Kate Middleton. Visitors can tour working traditional artisanal gem pits, where miners descend into narrow bamboo-shored shafts, haul up basketfuls of gem-bearing gravel, and wash the gravel in wicker baskets in nearby streams. In the town center, the National Museum of Ratnapura and private gem museums showcase rough and polished sapphires, rubies, padparadscha, and cat's eyes alongside prehistoric fossils of elephants, hippos, and rhinos discovered deep in gem pits.

Historical & Cultural Background

Mentioned by ancient Roman, Arab, and Persian traders (Sinbad the Sailor visited 'Serendib' for gems); described by Marco Polo in 1292.

Natural & Geographical Characteristics

Alluvial river floodplains and fertile gravel beds surrounded by mist-covered mountain ranges.

What Makes It Special

Traditional artisanal pit mining techniques (yathura), daily street gem market (Ambagahayata), and rare gemstone museum exhibits.

Why You Shouldn't Miss This Place

  • Stand at the epicenter of the global sapphire trade and watch traditional pit miners washing gem gravel
  • Witness the daily morning open-air street gem market in Ratnapura town
  • Admire priceless royal blue sapphires, rubies, and alexandrites in town museums
  • Learn how ancient alluvial geology concentrated earth's rarest treasures beneath this valley

Things to Do & Available Experiences

Gem mine pit tour
Street gem market observation
Visiting the National Museum of Ratnapura
Gemological education

Insider Travel Tips

What to Bring: Camera, comfortable shoes for muddy field trails around mines, magnifying loupe (if interested in gemology).
Recommended Clothing: Casual comfortable travel wear.
Photography Advice: Photograph miners washing the gem-bearing gravel (illam) in traditional circular conical wicker baskets in the river.
Local Customs: Always seek testing and certification at the official National Gem and Jewellery Authority office.
Eco & Conservation Guidelines: Modern regulations mandate that excavated gem pits must be refilled with soil to prevent environmental degradation.

Visitor Information

District Ratnapura District
Province Sabaragamuwa Province
Opening Hours Museums open 09:00 - 17:00; mines operate Monday to Saturday daylight hours.
Entrance Fee Nominal museum entrance fee: approx. LKR 300 - 500; mine visits arranged via local accredited operators.
Best Season Year-round; morning visits (08:30 - 11:30) allow you to catch the lively morning street gem market in action.
Visiting Duration 2 - 3 Hours
Accessibility Museums are accessible; gem pits are in working rural agricultural fields with dirt paths.
Safety Advice Do not purchase gemstones on the street from unlicensed peddlers; purchase only from certified members of the National Gem and Jewellery Authority with authenticity certificates.
