Madukanda Sri Dalada Viharaya

An ancient 4th-century CE forest temple marking the sacred resting place of the Tooth Relic on its overland journey from India to Anuradhapura.

Temples & Religious Sites
Vavuniya District
Best Season: Year-round; morning visits provide peaceful contemplation.
Duration: 1 Hour
Hidden Gem

About Madukanda Sri Dalada Viharaya

Situated in a tranquil forest setting 5 kilometers outside Vavuniya town, Madukanda Sri Dalada Viharaya is a deeply venerated historical sanctuary. According to historical chronicles, when Prince Danta and Princess Hemamala brought the sacred Tooth Relic of the Buddha from Kalinga, India, to Sri Lanka in the 4th century CE during the reign of King Kithsirimewan, this temple served as their first major resting point on their journey south toward the royal capital of Anuradhapura. The temple complex features ancient ruined stone pillars, a circular Moonstone, ruined stupa foundations, and an ancient sacred Bo tree shaded by giant tropical forest trees.

Historical & Cultural Background

Dating back to the 4th century CE; commemorated as the historic Tooth Relic transit station.

Natural & Geographical Characteristics

Tranquil forest hermitage setting adjacent to an ancient village irrigation reservoir.

What Makes It Special

Ancient stone pillar ruins, Moonstone carving, historic stupa mound, and peaceful forest atmosphere.

Why You Shouldn't Miss This Place

  • Stand at the historical forest sanctuary that sheltered the sacred Tooth Relic in the 4th century CE
  • Explore ancient carved stone pillars and moonstones in a peaceful woodland setting
  • Escape the highway into an authentic, serene heritage monastery
  • Learn about the legendary journey of Prince Danta and Princess Hemamala

Things to Do & Available Experiences

Temple exploration
Historical reflection
Ancient stone ruin photography

Insider Travel Tips

What to Bring: Modest clothing, slip-off footwear.
Recommended Clothing: White or light-colored attire covering shoulders and knees.
Photography Advice: Photograph the ancient moss-covered stone pillars framed by towering canopy trees.
Local Customs: Quiet respectful reverence in sacred temple areas.
Eco & Conservation Guidelines: Preserve the natural tranquility of the forest monastery.
