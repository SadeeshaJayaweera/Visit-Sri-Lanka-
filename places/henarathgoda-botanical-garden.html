Henarathgoda Botanical Garden

A historic 43-acre botanical garden where the first Brazilian Para rubber seedlings were planted in 1876, sparking South Asia's rubber industry.

Botanical Gardens
Gampaha District
Best Season: October to April; morning hours are cool and tranquil.
Duration: 1.5 - 3 Hours
Hidden Gem

About Henarathgoda Botanical Garden

Situated in Gampaha, the Henarathgoda Botanical Garden holds a pivotal place in Asian economic history. Established in 1876 by the British botanist Sir Henry Wickham, who smuggled Para rubber seeds (Hevea brasiliensis) from the Amazon rainforest to Kew Gardens in London, this garden was chosen to plant the first surviving rubber seedlings in South Asia. From these historic trees, rubber cultivation spread across Sri Lanka, Malaysia, and the continent. Today, the peaceful 43-acre botanical park features sprawling palms, an orchidarium, a scenic lake with paddleboats, towering bamboo groves, and the preserved stump of Rubber Tree No. 2.

Historical & Cultural Background

Established in 1876 specifically to receive and cultivate the first Amazon rubber saplings imported via the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew.

Natural & Geographical Characteristics

Lowland wet-zone arboretum, flowering gardens, and freshwater lake.

What Makes It Special

Historic birthplace of the Asian rubber industry, towering rain trees, and tranquil boat lake.

Why You Shouldn't Miss This Place

  • Stand at the historical birthplace of South Asia's multi-billion dollar rubber industry
  • Stroll along uncrowded pathways beneath giant century-old tropical canopy trees
  • Rent a paddleboat on the serene garden lake with family or friends
  • Admire rare tropical orchids and indigenous medicinal plant collections

Insider Travel Tips

What to Bring: Comfortable footwear, insect repellent, drinking water, camera.
Recommended Clothing: Casual park wear.
Photography Advice: Capture the majestic avenue of royal palms and the buttress roots of giant rain trees.
Local Customs: A serene, quiet retreat popular with local botanical students.
Eco & Conservation Guidelines: Do not pick flowers or damage tree bark.
