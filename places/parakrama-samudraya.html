Parakrama Samudraya (Sea of Parakrama)

The colossal 2,500-hectare ancient inland reservoir engineered by King Parakramabahu the Great, fulfilling his decree that 'not a single drop of rain must flow to the sea unutilized'.

About Parakrama Samudraya (Sea of Parakrama)

Dominating the western horizon of Polonnaruwa, the Parakrama Samudraya (Sea of Parakrama) is an awe-inspiring triumph of ancient hydraulic engineering. Created in the 12th century CE by King Parakramabahu the Great by merging five previously existing smaller reservoirs, this vast inland reservoir spans over 2,500 hectares (9.6 square miles) with an immense 14-kilometer-long earthen embankment rising up to 12 meters high. Engineered under the king's famous royal decree that 'not even a single drop of water that falls on this earth as rain must be allowed to flow into the ocean without being useful to man', the reservoir continues to irrigate over 18,000 hectares of paddy fields today. The scenic road along the top of the bund offers breathtaking sunset vistas across the vast water surface toward the distant mountain ranges of Knuckles and Matale, accompanied by waterbirds and the nearby 12th-century colossal rock statue of King Parakramabahu holding a palm-leaf manuscript.

Historical & Cultural Background

Constructed in the 12th century CE by King Parakramabahu I; restored in the 1940s by Prime Minister D.S. Senanayake as part of the agricultural colonization schemes.

Natural & Geographical Characteristics

Immense artificial freshwater lake and inland sea surrounded by agricultural plains and distant mountain views.

What Makes It Special

14-kilometer embankment bund, 2,500-hectare water surface, colossal statue of King Parakramabahu, and evening sunset vistas.

Why You Shouldn't Miss This Place

  • Stand before the crowning engineering achievement of ancient Sri Lankan hydraulic civilization
  • Take an evening stroll or bicycle ride along the 14-kilometer bund watching colorful sunset skies
  • Visit the nearby colossal 12th-century rock-cut statue of King Parakramabahu holding a book
  • Spot pelicans, cormorants, and sea eagles feeding in the vast inland waters

Things to Do & Available Experiences

Scenic bund walking and cycling
Sunset photography across the water
Visiting the King Parakramabahu statue and Potgul Vihara
Birdwatching

Insider Travel Tips

What to Bring: Camera, binoculars, light windbreaker for breezy evenings.
Recommended Clothing: Casual comfortable travel attire.
Photography Advice: Photograph the glowing orange sunset reflecting on the vast water surface with the distant Knuckles mountains on the horizon.
Local Customs: Enjoy fresh lake fish (lula or thilapia) prepared at local rest houses along the bund.
Eco & Conservation Guidelines: Keep the essential drinking and agricultural water reservoir clean.
