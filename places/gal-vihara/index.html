Gal Vihara (Rock Temple)

The supreme masterpiece of ancient Sri Lankan stone carving: four colossal Buddha statues hewn directly from a single massive granite cliff face by King Parakramabahu I.

Ancient Cities
Polonnaruwa District
Best Season: Early morning (07:30 - 09:30) or late afternoon (16:00 - 17:30) when light softly illuminates the granite facial contours.
Duration: 1 - 1.5 Hours
Popular Highlight

About Gal Vihara (Rock Temple)

Universally recognized as the pinnacle of ancient Sinhalese rock-cut sculpture and one of the supreme artistic achievements of Buddhist Asia, Gal Vihara (originally named Uttararama or 'Northern Monastery') is located in the northern sector of ancient Polonnaruwa. Commissioned in the 12th century CE by King Parakramabahu the Great, four monumental Buddha figures were sculpted directly out of a single continuous sloping granite boulder, each exhibiting astonishing artistic refinement, fluid drapery, and deep spiritual serenity. The figures include a colossal 4.6-meter seated Buddha in deep meditation on a vajrasana (diamond throne); an intricately carved cave-shrine Buddha (Vidyadhara Guha); an extraordinary 7-meter standing figure with crossed arms (believed by scholars to depict either the Buddha in supreme compassion or his chief disciple Ananda mourning); and a magnificent 14-meter colossal reclining Buddha depicting the Parinirvana (passing away of the Buddha), with exquisite details including the pillow indented under the head and the slightly recessed left foot signifying final release.

Historical & Cultural Background

Sculpted in the 12th century CE under the direct patronage of King Parakramabahu the Great; inscribed on the central rock face with the Polonnaruwa Katikawata (monastic code).

Natural & Geographical Characteristics

Monolithic sloping granite rock cliff situated in parkland shaded by ancient trees.

What Makes It Special

Four colossal rock-cut Buddha masterpieces, 14-meter reclining Buddha, 7-meter standing figure with crossed arms, and ancient royal stone inscription.

Why You Shouldn't Miss This Place

  • Stand before the supreme masterwork of ancient Sri Lankan granite stone sculpture
  • Marvel at the 14-meter reclining Buddha depicting the serene transition to Parinirvana
  • Admire the unique 7-meter standing figure with crossed arms expressing profound compassion
  • Contemplate centuries of spiritual peace in one of Asia's most revered sacred open-air shrines

Things to Do & Available Experiences

Masterpiece sculpture appreciation
Quiet contemplation and meditation
Historical stone inscription study
Photography

Insider Travel Tips

What to Bring: Thick socks to protect feet from hot sand/stone, modest clothing, camera (no flash), drinking water.
Recommended Clothing: Modest clothing covering shoulders and knees; white attire is warmly appreciated.
Photography Advice: Capture the serene facial profile of the 14-meter reclining Buddha showing the realistic indentation of the granite stone pillow.
Local Customs: Maintain complete reverence and silence in the presence of these venerated masterpieces.
Eco & Conservation Guidelines: Strictly do not touch or lean against the ancient carved granite rock surfaces.
