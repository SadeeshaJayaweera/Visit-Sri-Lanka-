Minneriya National Park (The Elephant Gathering)

Home of 'The Gathering', ranked by Lonely Planet as one of the world's top ten wildlife spectacles, where up to 300 wild Asian elephants assemble on the dry lakebed.

Duration: 3 - 4 Hours (Afternoon Safari)

About Minneriya National Park (The Elephant Gathering)

Spanning 8,889 hectares in the North Central dry zone, Minneriya National Park is globally renowned for hosting one of the greatest natural wildlife phenomena on Earth: 'The Elephant Gathering'. Centered around the ancient Minneriya Tank—a colossal artificial reservoir built in the 3rd century CE by King Mahasena (King of the Tank Builders)—the park transforms during the dry season (July to October). As surrounding regional waterholes dry up, water recedes in the reservoir, uncovering vast expanses of lush, fertile, fresh green grasses along the lakebed. Drawn from across the Matale, Polonnaruwa, and Trincomalee forests, between 150 and 300 wild Asian elephants (Elephas maximus maximus) converge in large family herds to feed, drink, play, bathe, and socialize in the open. Safari jeeps gather along the shoreline, offering spectators unmatched views of elephant matriarchs leading tiny calves, young tuskers mock-fighting, and immense herds interacting right before their eyes.

Historical & Cultural Background

Centered around the ancient Minneriya Tank built by King Mahasena in the 3rd century CE; declared a wildlife sanctuary in 1938 and upgraded to a National Park in 1997.

Natural & Geographical Characteristics

Lowland dry-zone semi-evergreen forest, bamboo thickets, and expansive grassland lakebeds of the Minneriya reservoir.

What Makes It Special

The Elephant Gathering (largest recurring congregation of wild Asian elephants in the world), ancient 3rd-century reservoir, and large waterbird colonies.

Why You Shouldn't Miss This Place

  • Witness 'The Gathering', where up to 300 wild Asian elephants congregate in the open in a single afternoon
  • Watch elephant calves playing and bathing in the lake while family herds socialize
  • Experience one of Lonely Planet's 'Top 10 Wildlife Spectacles in the World'
  • Observe large flocks of painted storks, spot-billed pelicans, and grey herons along the water's edge

Things to Do & Available Experiences

Afternoon 4x4 open-top jeep safaris
Wildlife and elephant herd photography
Birdwatching on the lakebed

Insider Travel Tips

What to Bring: Binoculars, telephoto camera lens (70-300mm), dust mask or buff, sun hat, sunglasses, drinking water.
Recommended Clothing: Neutral, earth-toned lightweight clothing (khaki, olive, beige).
Photography Advice: Photograph playful baby elephants sheltered between the legs of adult matriarchs against the golden afternoon lake light.
Local Customs: Choose ethical safari drivers who maintain respectful distances and do not encircle elephant herds.
Eco & Conservation Guidelines: Strict zero-litter policy; do not discard plastic bottles or food packaging.

Visitor Information

District Polonnaruwa District
Province North Central Province (Rajarata)
Opening Hours 06:00 - 18:00 Daily (afternoon safaris from 14:00 are strongly recommended).
Entrance Fee National Park ticket: approx. LKR 10,000 - 12,000 per foreign adult visitor (including park entry, vehicle pass, and VAT; 4x4 safari jeep hire approx. LKR 14,000 - 20,000).
Best Season July to October (peak dry season for 'The Elephant Gathering'); afternoon safaris (14:30 - 18:30) are essential as elephants emerge from the forest from 15:30 onwards.
Visiting Duration 3 - 4 Hours (Afternoon Safari)
Accessibility All exploration is conducted via customized 4x4 safari jeeps with elevated tiered seating.
Safety Advice Remain inside the safari vehicle at all times; do not stand on jeep roofs or shout; wild elephant herds can charge if provoked.
